# Low oil pressure: Error code 1300

## Environment

This article applies to error code **1300, LOW OIL PRESSURE**, for **Generac air-cooled home standby generators**.

> **Note**: The term "error code" is used interchangeably with 'alarm code,' 'fault code,' 'fault alarm' and similar terminology. Error code applies to all such terminology.

## Overview

- **Error code**: 1300
- **LED Indicator:** Red
- **Displayed Alarm**: LOW OIL PRESSURE
- **Issue**: The low oil pressure sensor has triggered a shutdown due to inadequate oil levels.
- **Cause**: The low oil pressure alarm is typically caused by low oil levels due to oil consumption, leaks, or lack of maintenance.

## What can I do?

- Your options are limited beyond performing basic checks on components and verifying that everything appears normal. You can attempt to clear the alarm and manually restart the unit.
- Sometimes, clearing the alarm and attempting to restart the unit is all that is needed, as the alarm may be a one-time occurrence that does not indicate a persistent issue.

## When should you contact a Generac independent Authorized Service Dealer (IASD)?

- If issues persist after performing the checks and attempting to restart, it is strongly recommended that you contact a Generac IASD for diagnosis.

## General troubleshooting

**1\.** Allow the generator to cool.

![](https://support.generac.com/servlet/rtaImage?eid=ka0US0000008kSH&feoid=00N5f00000gWhDU&refid=0EMU100000B7kar)

Before proceeding, ensure the generator has cooled for at least 30 minutes to avoid potential injury or inaccurate readings.

2\. Check and refill oil.

![](https://support.generac.com/servlet/rtaImage?eid=ka0US0000008kSH&feoid=00N5f00000gWhDU&refid=0EMU100000B7dKt)

Confirm that oil dipstick is fully inserted, and that the oil fill cap, oil drain cap, and oil filter are hand-tightened. Rotate clockwise to tighten. (righty-tight, left-loose). If any of these components are not tightened securely, leaks can occur.

- [How Do I Check the Oil Level in My Home Standby Generator?](https://support.generac.com/s/article/How-Do-I-Check-the-Oil-Level-in-My-Home-Standby-Generator)
- [How do I remove the front panel on my home standby generator?](https://support.generac.com/s/article/How-do-I-remove-the-front-panel-on-my-home-standby-generator)
- [What is the oil capacity of a home standby generator?](https://support.generac.com/s/article/What-is-the-oil-capacity-of-a-home-standby-generator)

3\. Check for oil leaks.

Inspect the generator for any visible oil leaks and address them as necessary.

- See the [How Do I Remove the Generator Side Panel?](https://support.generac.com/s/article/How-Do-I-Remove-the-Generator-Side-Panel) article to remove the side panel and inspect for oil leaks. If you see evidence of a leak, clean it up first and dispose of used oil according to national, state, or local codes.
- [How to check lines and connections for fuel or oil leaks](https://support.generac.com/s/article/How-to-check-lines-and-connections-for-fuel-or-oil-leaks)

4\. Clear the alarm and reset.

- [How Do I Clear an Error or Alarm Code On My Home Standby Generator](https://support.generac.com/s/article/How-Do-I-Clear-an-Error-On-My-Home-Standby-Generator)
- For [Evolution](https://support.generac.com/s/article/What-is-the-Evolution-Control-Panel-for-Home-Standby-Generators) controllers: Press OFF, ENTER twice, then AUTO.
- For [Power Zone 200](https://support.generac.com/s/article/What-is-the-Power-Zone-200-Controller-for-Generac-home-standby-generators) controllers: Hold the OFF button for 3 seconds.
- **Important**: Never start the generator while under load. See below troubleshooting by scenario [Outage/utility power failure](https://support.generac.com/s/article/What-Does-It-Mean-When-My-Generator-Displays-an-Error-Code-Low-Oil-Pressure-Code-1300#outage) for more information on returning the generator to load while in an outage.

## Troubleshooting by scenario

Outage/utility power failure

- **Never start a unit while under load.**
- [How Do I Shut Down My Generator When It’s Powering My House (During an Outage)?](https://support.generac.com/s/article/How-Do-I-Shut-Down-My-Generator-When-It-s-Powering-My-House)
- When running continuously for extended periods, such as in a significant outage event, the unit is required to be **shutdown every 24 hours**, for 30 minutes to allow the unit to cool and the oil levels to be checked.
- [Running your generator for extended periods during a major outage](https://support.generac.com/s/article/Running-your-generator-for-extended-periods-during-a-major-outage)

Error occurs intermittently

- [Troubleshooting intermittent or difficult to replicate alarms, faults, and error codes](https://support.generac.com/s/article/Troubleshooting-intermittent-or-difficult-to-replicate-alarms-faults-and-error-codes)

## Next steps: When troubleshooting works or fails

Troubleshooting worked

- If troubleshooting was successful, take note of the potential factors that may have contributed to the fault alarm.
- Considerations such as your run hours and upcoming scheduled maintenance intervals.

Troubleshooting failed

- If the issue remains unresolved after attempting the outlined checks and troubleshooting, contact a Generac IASD for diagnosis.

Working with a Generac IASD for diagnosis and repair

- Only a Generac IASD can submit warranty claims and perform warranty repairs.
- Leave the error code present on the unit so it can be properly diagnosed.
- Keep records of all maintenance performed.
- Note any situational factors or details related to the alarm occurrence; this information may help the technician diagnose the issue more effectively.
